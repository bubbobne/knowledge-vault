# Notes on *Moore (1985)*  
*The Probability-Distributed Principle and Runoff Production at Point and Basin Scales*

---

## 🟦 Executive Summary

Moore (1985) introduces a probabilistic representation of soil moisture storage across a catchment.  
Instead of assuming that all points have the same storage capacity, the catchment is represented by a **distribution of capacities**.  
Runoff is generated when rainfall exceeds the local storage capacity.

The key result is an analytical expression for **runoff generated by a rainfall pulse $P$**, given the **antecedent mean storage $S_n$** and the **capacity distribution parameters**.

---


The catchment is viewed as a continuum of point-scale “reservoirs,” each with a different storage capacity $c$.  
Runoff is generated at points where rainfall exceeds the local capacity.  
If we sort capacities in ascending order, the threshold capacity - criticxal capacity $(C^{*}(t))$ defines the portion of the catchment that is saturated at time $(t)$.

The distribution of capacities is described by:

$$
F(C^*(t)) = P(c \le C^*(t)) = \int_0^{C^*(t)} f(c)\,dc
$$

where:

- $f(c)$ is the probability density of capacities  
- $F(c)$ is the cumulative distribution  
- $F(C^*(t))$ = fraction of the catchment that is saturated

And the contributing area:

$$
A_c(t) = A\,F(C^*(t))
$$

If rainfall intensity $\pi$ is constant during the event, then instantaneous runoff is:

$$
q(t) = \pi\,F(C^*(t))
$$

Under constant rainfall intensity:

$$
C^*(\tau) = C^*(t) + \pi(\tau - t)
$$

This expresses how the saturation threshold rises in time during rainfall. Then the runoff volume generated in the interval $[t, t+\Delta t]$ is:

$$
V(t+\Delta t)
=
\int_t^{t+\Delta t} q(\tau)\,d\tau
=
\int_t^{t+\Delta t} \pi\,F(C^*(\tau))\,d\tau.
$$

### **Change of variable**

Let:

$$
c = C^*(\tau).
$$

From Formula 4:

$$
c = C^*(t) + \pi(\tau - t)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
d\tau = \frac{1}{\pi}\,dc.
$$

Limits change from:

$$
\tau \in [t, t+\Delta t]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c \in [C^*(t),\, C^*(t+\Delta t)].
$$

Thus:

$$
V(t+\Delta t)
= 
\pi \int_{C^*(t)}^{C^*(t+\Delta t)} F(c)\,\frac{1}{\pi}\,dc
$$

$$
\boxed{
V(t+\Delta t)
=
\int_{C^*(t)}^{C^*(t+\Delta t)} F(c)\,dc
}
$$

When working with discrete timesteps, the rainfall input cannot simply be applied as a single lumped value.  
If the entire rainfall amount for the timestep is added at once, it may immediately drive the catchment to full saturation, which is inconsistent with the continuous formulation of the model.  
In such cases, the computation must be split into at least two sub-intervals: one from t to the time when $C^∗(t)$ reaches saturation, and another for the remaining rainfall.

The same issue appears when using a finite $C_{\max}$​.  
If the timestep is not subdivided appropriately, the evolution equation:

$$
C∗(\tau)=C^∗(t)+π(\tau−t)C^*(\tau)
$$

will exceed Cmax⁡C_{\max}Cmax​, which is not physically possible.  
Therefore, when rainfall intensity is large relative to the timestep, the model requires temporal discretization (sub-stepping) to ensure that$C^∗(t)$does not surpass its upper limit

---

###  Maximum possible storage

The maximum catchment storage is:

$$
S_{\max} = \int_0^\infty c\,f(c)\,dc.
$$

By integration by parts:

$$
\int_0^\infty c\,f(c)\,dc
=
\int_0^\infty (1 - F(c))\,dc.
$$
Because the local capacity ccc cannot exceed the upper limit $C_{\max}$​, the average storage of the basin must always be **strictly smaller** than ⁡$C_{\max}$.  
This follows directly from the fact that a mean value cannot exceed the maximum value of the support of its distribution

### **Explanation (why the equality holds)**

Using:

- $u = c$,  
- $dv = f(c)\,dc$ so $v = F(c)$:

$$
\int_0^\infty c f(c)\,dc
=
[cF(c)]_0^\infty - \int_0^\infty F(c)\,dc.
$$

Since $F(\infty)=1$, the boundary term gives $C_{\max}$, and both expressions are identical.

### **Real evapotranspiration**

Moore notes that real evapotranspiration is a complex physical process involving atmospheric demand, radiation, plant physiology, and soil water tension.  
To keep the Probability Distributed Model analytically tractable, he adopts a simple but effective assumption:

> **real evapotranspiration is proportional to the degree of saturation of the catchment.**  
> Less water → less actual ET.

This allows the model to update storage using only information about the cumulative distribution of capacitie

## **From independent reservoirs to redistribution**

Up to this point, each point-scale reservoir has been treated independently:  
rainfall fills each reservoir according to its own capacity ccc, and runoff occurs when rainfall exceeds that capacity.

However, modelling evapotranspiration requires a mechanism to redistribute water between reservoirs.  
Without redistribution, the spatial pattern of saturation would not evolve in a hydrologically consistent way.

Moore considers two possible assumptions:

1. **Equal deficit redistribution**
    
2. **Equal storage redistribution**
    

He chooses **equal storage redistribution**, because it greatly simplifies the model.

Under equal storage redistribution:

> After rainfall or evapotranspiration, all partially-filled reservoirs (those with $c>C∗c$ share water so that they all reach the same relative level of saturation.

This assumption preserves the analytic form of the equations and leads to the compact expressions used later in the model

## **Storage at time t**

$$
S(t)
=
\int_0^{C^*(t)} c f(c)\,dc
+
C^*(t)\int_{C^*(t)}^\infty f(c)\,dc.
$$

Interpretation:

- below $C^*(t)$: fully saturated points  
- above $C^*(t)$: partially filled points


### **Integration-by-parts identity**

$$
\int_0^x c f(c)\,dc
=
xF(x)-\int_0^x F(c)\,dc.
$$

This is explicitly derived by integration by parts.


### **Final compact expression using **

Using Formula previous yields:

$$
S(t)=\int_0^{C^*(t)} (1-F(c))\,dc.
$$

---
Moore notes that real evapotranspiration is a complex physical process involving atmospheric demand, radiation, plant physiology, and soil water tension.  
To keep the Probability Distributed Model analytically tractable, he adopts a simple but effective assumption:

> **real evapotranspiration is proportional to the degree of saturation of the catchment.**  
> Less water → less actual ET.

This allows the model to update storage using only information about the cumulative distribution of capacities.

# Pareto distribution

Moore notes that the **Pareto distribution** fits observed catchment variability best.

**Cumulative distribution**

$$
F(c)=1-\left(1-\frac{c}{C_{\max}}\right)^{b+1}
$$

 **Density**

$$
f(c)=\frac{b+1}{C_{\max}}
\left(1-\frac{c}{C_{\max}}\right)^{b}.
$$

Interpretation:

- $C_{\max}$ = maximum storage capacity  
- $b$ controls spatial variability  
  - $b=0$: uniform capacity across the catchment  
  - higher $b$: strong spatial heterogeneity
